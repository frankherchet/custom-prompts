# Light Beige Editorial Reference

Use this reference when the user wants a warm, intelligent, editorial deck with a softer paper-like tone rather than a cold corporate interface.

## Core Feel

- warm beige background
- soft paper-like surfaces
- editorial and thoughtful
- structured, but not cold
- technical content presented with more human texture

## Visual Language

- beige, bone, or parchment background tones
- dark neutral text
- one cool accent and optionally one muted warm accent
- visible grid or paper texture can work if subtle
- cards should feel like placed sheets or panels, not app windows

## Typography

- strong headline with some personality
- monospace for metadata and small technical inserts
- body copy slightly warmer and more essay-like than boardroom styles

## Layout Rules

- combine structure with atmosphere
- cards, terminal inserts, and labels should still align to a clear grid
- use warmer spacing and more breathing room than in OEH dark style
- keep the deck browsable like a designed document

## Copy Style

- analytical, deliberate, and compact
- less aggressive than dark technical
- more narrative than boardroom
- still avoid startup cliches and generic marketing language

## Components Worth Reusing

- warm paper panel
- label strip with subtle borders
- compact terminal inset
- flow arrow or sequence marker
- source block with thin left rule

## Failure Modes

- too much beige can feel retro or dusty
- warm palette without strong typography gets mushy
- editorial tone should not become vague or poetic

## Good Use Cases

- concept explainers
- internal memos turned into decks
- research synthesis
- calm but still designed technical storytelling
